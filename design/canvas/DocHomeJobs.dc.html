Jobs to be Done Canvas: Quilt Home

Job Overview

Job Title: Check in on my life in one glance Date: Nov 19, 2026 Author: Quilt founding team (drafted with Claude) Research Basis: Founders' pitch doc, persona PU-001 (docs/research/persona-sam-student.md, Proto confidence), launcher research (https://claude.ai/artifact/WtBMJUDese1MWWMTjFu8Dd). No interviews yet; every insight below is a hypothesis to test in Thursday's survey.

Job Performer

Who: Sam, a U of U undergrad who loses money and stress to what they forget, opening Quilt between other things.

Key Characteristics: - Opens Home 10+ times a day in 5 to 30 second bursts (assumed) - Has 5 to 30 apps they built themselves; no two students' Homes contain the same apps - Wants Home to say what matters so they don't have to go looking

Not Defined By: - Which apps they have (Home must work for any mix) - How organised they are

The Circumstance

When does this job arise? Opening Quilt with no specific task: waking up, between classes, after a purchase, before the gym, Sunday night.

Where does this happen? One-handed on a phone, often walking or in a line; on the laptop when planning.

Frequency: Many times a day.

Urgency: Low per visit, but a miss compounds (a renewal, a late fee, a deadline).

Job Statement

"When I open Quilt with a spare few seconds, I want to see whatever needs me, what's coming next and how I'm doing, without opening anything, so I can stop paying for what I forget and get on with my day."

Functional Job

What is the practical task to accomplish? Know in under 5 seconds whether anything needs action, then act in one tap.

Definition of "Done": - Anything waiting for Sam's OK is handled or knowingly left - The next 1 to 3 time-bound things are known - The one number Sam cares about right now is seen

Key Steps in the Job: 1. Scan: anything for me? 2. Act on it (confirm, dismiss, or open) 3. Glance at what's next 4. Jump into an app, or close Quilt

Functional Pains: - Every app is different, so Home can't show "the budget widget" for everyone - Too many apps makes Home a wall of tiles - Things that need action hide inside apps until it's too late

Competing Solutions

Why Do They Switch? A missed renewal or late fee; a dashboard they stopped updating.

Why Do They Stay? Widgets and notifications are already there; setting up something new feels like work.

Hiring Criteria

Must-Have (Table Stakes): - Works for any set of user-built apps - Shows what needs action first, in thumb reach - Fast: useful in under 5 seconds, no loading spinner

Differentiators (Decision Drivers): - Joins things up across apps ("Groceries spend is pushing Eating out over") - Changes with the time of day without Sam arranging it - Quiet when nothing is happening: "all caught up" is a good state

Nice-to-Have: - Small celebrations of real wins - Laptop version for Sunday planning

Insights and Implications

Key Insight 1: Home can't be made of app widgets, because every user's apps differ. Every app is a typed spec, so every app emits the same kinds of signals, and Home is built from those: things waiting for OK, dated items, a headline number, changes, and cross-app patterns. - Product Implication: every app spec declares a face (headline value + short label) and exposes dated rows and pending items. Home is composed from those signals, ranked by Clair.

Key Insight 2: The job runs in this order: act, next, glance, browse. - Product Implication: Home sections follow that order: Waiting for you, Next up, Right now, Clair noticed, Your quilt. Empty sections disappear entirely.

Key Insight 3: "Nothing to do" must feel like success. - Product Implication: an explicit caught-up state with the spool content, and no counts or badges on patches except "just changed".

Key Insight 4: One status sentence written from live data does more than five cards. - Product Implication: a composed status line under the greeting ("Pull day in 15 minutes. $376 left this month.") in plain, specific words that never judge.
