# Quilt: one page

**Problem.** Students managing money for the first time pay a "forgetting tax": renewals they forgot to cancel, late fees, groceries that go off, deadlines that sneak up. Logging it all takes longer than the purchase did, so they fall back on the free Notes app.

**Customer.** Full-time University of Utah undergrads, 18 to 24, phone-first, who won't connect a bank account.

**Offer and price.** One photo of a receipt, a syllabus or a fridge updates budget, groceries and due dates after one tap; no bank login. Free tier on a low-cost model; Student Plus at $12 a semester ($3 a month); a campus plan paid by the university (to test).

| Number | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Contribution per paying student-month | $1.79 (60%) at $3, capture on Claude Haiku 5.5 |
| Same at the pitched $6, Opus 5.5 on every action | −$17.18 |
| Break-even | 46 paying students |
| Year 1 operating profit (500 paying by month 12) | $3,249 on $7,080 revenue |
| Cash needed | $426; earned back in month 7 |

**Evidence.**
- **Buyer panel:** 0 of 20 simulated buyers paid at $6. The acceptable range is $1 to $4 a month.
- **Competitors:** the gap is that no app turns one photo into updates across money, groceries and deadlines without a bank login. It's a missing feature, not a moat.
- **Real tests:** none yet.

**Verdict: Not yet.** The unit economics work at a student price with a cheap capture model. Demand at any price is unproven.

**What the money is for.** About $426: hosting, store accounts and a campus waitlist push.

**The ask.** 50 U of U students on a waitlist at $12 a semester, and an answer to "Would you use it if the U paid for it?"

The panel is simulated buyers and the numbers are projections.
