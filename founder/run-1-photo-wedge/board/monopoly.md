# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **A small market you can name.** Full-time U of U undergrads managing money for the first time is narrow and reachable. That is the right size to take a big share of first.
- **A possible 10x.** One photo updating Budget, Groceries and Calendar in one tap, against typing into three apps, could be ten times faster on what this buyer cares about most: logging faster than the purchase took. No bank login is an edge over Rocket Money.
- **Timing.** Photo-to-structured-data only recently became cheap and reliable.
- **Switching costs.** Buying history and linked apps grow monthly.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **No proprietary advantage; the model is rented.** Anyone can call the same AI. Notion, Google and Apple already own the phone and the notes app and could ship "photo fills your list" as a feature. Nothing stops a copycat in month six. Draft-and-confirm is good design, not a moat.
2. **Seven fights at once.** Budget, Groceries, Classes, To-dos, Workout, Subscriptions and Calendar each have a free incumbent. Competition destroys profit; this plan picks seven fights instead of winning one.
3. **Margins depend on a supplier.** Every AI action costs money, and the API cost does not fall as Quilt grows. AI cost per active user is unmeasured (the CFO step answers it); $6 a month may not cover it.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- What is the secret: what do you know about this student that Notion and Google do not act on?
- Which single loop do you own first? Receipt to Budget plus Groceries looks strongest, so why seven apps?
- How do the first 100 users arrive, and what does each cost? No waitlist or survey numbers exist yet (consumer panel).
- Is there a network effect? Do roommates sharing a list or budget pull each other in? The marketplace needs creators and buyers at once.
- Will students pay when every competitor has a free tier? (consumer panel, competitor scout)

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. Narrow to one wedge, such as receipt to Budget plus Groceries, and win it at one campus first.
2. Measure capture time against the next-best option and show close to 10x, not 2x.
3. Name the durable advantage being built (shared-household network effect, owned buying-history data, or campus brand) and show first evidence.
4. CFO confirms AI cost per active user at the planned credit limits leaves a margin at $6 a month.
5. Show the distribution path: U of U waitlist sign-ups and cost per user.
