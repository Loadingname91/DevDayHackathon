# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **The capture moment.** Photo of a whiteboard or receipt, a draft lands in the right folder, one tap to approve, and it is already on the laptop. You can picture it, and most of the value sits there.
- **Nothing changes without an OK.** A taste decision that protects trust where AI products usually lose it.
- **The one-liner works:** "Notion makes you build it. Quilt builds it for you and fills itself."
- **One build for phone and laptop**, synced live, closes the seam between devices that students live across.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **No focus.** Notes, journal, workouts, cars, plants, budget, groceries, connectors, automations, webhooks, teams and a marketplace make a list of products, not a product. Students drop apps that need setup, and a blank "describe your app" box is still setup. Without one hero job, the first five minutes have no shape.
2. **Seams in the core loop.** Camera, AI, the right app and folder, the draft, the approval, then sync: each hop can break (wrong folder, misread total, a laptop that has not caught up). Draft accuracy has not been measured. If students fix drafts often, "fills itself" becomes a chore.
3. **The meter sits inside the magic.** A photo fill costs 5 credits, so Free gets about ten a month, and the best moment can stop with "Clair paused." Rationing the delight shows a seam to exactly the users it needs to convert. Whether the credit numbers are right depends on AI cost per user, which is unknown; the CFO step answers it.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- If Quilt could do one job brilliantly next term, which is it?
- Walk me through a new student's first five minutes, tap by tap. When do they say "oh"?
- What are you cutting today?
- How often is Clair's draft wrong, and what does fixing it feel like? (Evals on real photos answer this.)
- Which app does a student still open after a month? (Unknown; the consumer panel answers it.)
- What does a student see when credits run out mid-photo?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. Pick one hero job (class-notes photo to a filed note on the laptop, or receipt to budget) and build onboarding around it alone.
2. Drop the marketplace, Teams, webhooks, bring-your-own-key and automations from the near-term plan.
3. Measure draft accuracy and 30-day app retention before adding blocks.
4. Get AI cost per active user from the CFO step, then set credits so the hero job never stops mid-task.
