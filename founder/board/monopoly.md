# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market with walls.** One campus is closed, dense and reachable through student groups and shared classes. A big share of it is realistic.
- **Linked data compounds.** Once classes, budget, calendar and notes feed each other, leaving costs more every month. That switching cost is the best moat on offer.
- **Timing.** Models that read a receipt or syllabus photo well enough to fill records are recent, and the draft-and-approve step makes them trustworthy.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proprietary edge.** The intelligence is Claude, which any competitor can call. Notion, Base44, Taskade and Glide already build from prompts. If Notion adds "build a tracker from this photo", the "Notion makes you build it" pitch dies within months.
2. **Horizontal product, small market.** Workouts, plants, cars, journals and budgets each face a different free app. Owning a small market needs a clear win at one job; "everything for students" wins at none.
3. **Margins squeezed both ways.** Every Clair action costs money while substitutes are free. AI cost per active user and free-to-paid conversion are unknown, so a $6 plan could lose money on heavy users. The CFO step answers this.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one job does Quilt do ten times better than what U of U students use today, in minutes or steps saved?
- What do you know that Notion does not? "Students hate setup" is common knowledge.
- What stops Notion or Base44 copying photo-to-draft in month six, and what will you have by then that they cannot copy?
- How do the first 100 students arrive, and what does each cost? (No waitlist or survey numbers yet.)
- After a month, how many apps does a student still use, and do links between apps raise that? (The consumer panel answers this.)
- Where does a network effect come from: shared class apps, the marketplace, or Teams?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Narrow the beachhead** to one job, such as syllabus and lecture photos into a class tracker plus calendar for U of U courses, and win it before adding apps.
2. **Confirm the gap.** The competitor scout shows no incumbent does photo-to-approved-draft across linked apps for students.
3. **Prove the switching cost.** The consumer panel shows linked apps retain better after a month than standalone ones.
4. **Prove the margin.** The CFO shows AI cost per active user below plan revenue, with credits capping heavy users.
5. **Name the network effect** to build first, such as classmates sharing a course app.
