# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Effort and time score best.** "Snap the syllabus or receipt, tap OK" asks almost nothing and pays off in seconds.
- **Buyers are easy to reach.** U of U student groups, one campus, a waitlist page: a cheap, narrow channel.
- **Real urgency exists.** Semester start is honest scarcity.
- **A stack already exists.** Capture from anywhere, auto-sorting, phone-to-laptop sync and shared data between apps each solve a separate problem.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A market weak on money.** Students have the pain; whether they spend on it is open. Free options are everywhere (Notes, Google Docs, Obsidian, Notion's free tier), and there is no evidence yet that students will pay. A weak market is this lens's most common reason to pass. The consumer panel answers this.
2. **It sells a tool, not an outcome.** Nobody wants an app builder. They want to stay on top of ECON 1010, stay under budget, stop losing notes. With no named dream outcome and no proof, the value equation collapses and the case against Notion shrinks to "less setup", which is easy to copy.
3. **Price set before cost.** Every Clair action costs money, credits are priced 1/5/10, and AI cost per active user is unmeasured. Heavy users could lose money, and credits add effort for the buyer to understand. The CFO answers this.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which single outcome would a student pay $6 for in week one?
- What proof will you show: one real student's semester, before and after?
- How many minutes to a new user's first win?
- What is the guarantee? For example: "semester set up from your syllabi in 10 minutes, or Plus is free for the term."
- Do competitors already give students their paid tier free? (Competitor scout.)
- Could you charge $12 and still be the obvious choice? If not, what is missing from the stack?
- What does one credit cost you to deliver? (CFO.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Lead with one named outcome ("your semester, organized from a photo"); the app builder is how it is delivered, not what is sold.
2. The consumer panel shows a stated share of students paying at a stated price before tiers are fixed.
3. The CFO shows each credit costs less to deliver than it earns at every tier, including the free 50.
4. Add a real guarantee and a semester-start offer with an honest deadline.
5. Show outcomes, not credit counts, on the pricing page.
