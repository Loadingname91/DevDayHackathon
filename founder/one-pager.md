# Quilt: one page

**Problem.** People 18 to 35 run their notes, classes, money, workouts and life admin across five or more apps that don't share data. The one app they actually need usually doesn't exist.

**Customer.** Students first (U of U undergrads and grad students), then young professionals and freelancers.

**Offer and price.**
- **The product:** describe any app and Clair builds it. It's filled from anywhere: typing, voice, a photo, a file or a connector. Nothing changes until the user taps OK, and everything syncs between phone and laptop.
- **Free:** 5 apps, with free import and export.
- **Plus:** $6 a month. Student Plus is $4.
- **Pro:** $12 a month, with connectors.

| Number | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Contribution per paying user-month | $3.24 (54%) at $6, with Haiku 5.5 for capture and Sonnet 5.5 for builds |
| Same with Opus 5.5 on every action | −$19.58 |
| Break-even | 26 paying users |
| Year 1 operating profit (500 paying by month 12) | $6,671 on $14,160 revenue |
| Cash needed | $374; earned back in month 5 |

**Evidence.**
- **Buyer panel:** 0 of 20 simulated buyers bought. $6 sits inside their acceptable range ($1.02 to $6.99), and 15 passed on switching cost.
- **Competitors:** no app lets someone describe an app on an Android phone, has the AI build it and fill it from a photo at a student price, with nothing written until approved. That gap is narrow: Taskade Genesis builds apps from one sentence for $6.
- **Real tests:** none yet.

**Verdict: Not yet.** The unit economics work with the model split. Demand is unproven, and switching cost is the wall.

**What the money is for.** About $374: hosting, store accounts and a waitlist push.

**The ask.** 50 sign-ups on a waitlist at a real price, and a count of "Import from Notion / Notes / Obsidian" clicks.

The panel is simulated buyers and the numbers are projections.
